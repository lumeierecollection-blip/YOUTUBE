# Content spec: how a topic, a title and a script are chosen so they match what wins

Written 2026-11-14 after the owner's correction: length and pacing were not the problem; the topic, the writing, the title and what the video gives off did not match the winning content. Evidence: `WINNERS-AND-SOURCING.md`, `research/topicdb.json` (RealLifeLore, 250 titles), `research/competitors.json` (AI Search, Matt Wolfe), our own retention report (`SHORTS-ANALYSIS-AND-FIXES.md`).

## What the winners are (measured)
- **RealLifeLore** (our Hidden Past reference): "Why X ..." about something large and consequential. Top 40 titles by multiple of the channel's median: 63% start with "Why" (bottom 60: 40%); 28% say empty/hidden/secret/nobody (bottom: 8%). Top titles: *How North Korea Finally Made It Impossible to Escape* 17.5x, *Why Russia is Invading Ukraine* 17.5x, *Why 95% of Australia is Empty* 11.7x, *What Happens If Yellowstone Blows Up Tomorrow?* 10.8x. The fixed reference (*Why The Nepal Flood Was WAY Scarier Than You Think*, 2.33M views) is a current event, opened on real footage, then "the most terrifying part is how it began".
- **AI Search / Matt Wolfe** (AI Tested reference): the newest model or tool plus a reaction or a payoff: *GPT-5 is a freak* 4.9x, *Deepseek strikes again*, *Claude Opus 5.5 is ridiculous*, *27 FREE Things You Can Do With Gemini* 4.5x, *GPT-6 Astra Is Finally Here* 3.8x.
- **Our own AI Tested Shorts**: the best were about whether to trust AI, as a conflict (*We Told AI It Was Wrong. It Didn't Budge.* 1,612 views).

## The gate: a topic is built only if all four are true (every item from fetched data, never from memory)
1. **A live trigger or a proven pattern.** A real event in the last 30 days (a model release, a crisis, a disaster) found in a dated source, or a paradox pattern with a measured track record ("why X% of Y is empty", "what happens if", "what's hidden").
2. **A stake in one sentence**, stated about the viewer's world, not about the past ("a quarter of the world's oil runs through one strait and it is closed").
3. **Primary or reference sources and lawful pictures exist** (public-domain or CC footage and photographs, our own data), found before writing.
4. **Competitor proof**: a video on the same subject or pattern did at least about twice its channel's median.

## The script (thesis, not chronology)
- Open on the stake with a real picture, in the first sentence. Never open on set-up or on "today we look at".
- One thesis, escalated: three surprises, one reversal, then a callback to the opening image. Second person where it helps.
- Every number sourced and shown with its source on screen; contested matters (who is to blame) are given as each party's stated position.
- No encyclopaedic list of dates; each date has to change what the viewer thinks.

## Title and thumbnail
- Ten title candidates per topic, scored against the winners' features: a "why / last time / what happens" thesis, a concrete figure or object, a stake, no neutral description. Reject any that merely names the subject.
- Thumbnail: one dominant real subject, one to four words, one symbol (circle, arrow, stamp). No drawn chart as the main image.
