## 3. RESEARCH THE CURRENT YOUTUBE MARKET

Look for:

- Exceptional viewership.
- Fast-growing videos.

- Highly discussed subjects.
- Emerging topics.
- Strong audience engagement.
- Repeatable competitor success.

Research Shorts and long-form independently.

Only use faceless content as the production reference.

Record the actual video URLs, publication dates, view counts and available performance evidence.

Never invent statistics or claim to have watched a video that you could not access.

## 5. USE THE ACTUAL RESEARCHED VISUALS AS THE CREATIVE BLUEPRINT

This is a critical instruction.

Do not research a competitor's visual identity and then generate something unrelated.

The actual researched reference material must inform the visual production.

For every selected video:

1. Collect representative visual references from the competitor video.
2. Document their timestamps.
3. Identify their visual characteristics.
4. Establish the corresponding production specifications.
5. Create original visual assets following those specifications.
6. Compare the new assets against the reference.
7. Correct visual inconsistencies before proceeding.

The final production must reflect the researched visual direction.

For example:

If the reference uses cinematic archival footage, do not suddenly introduce generic cartoon illustrations.

If it uses realistic geographical maps, do not substitute unrelated stock footage.

If it uses detailed editorial motion graphics, do not replace them with basic slideshow transitions.

If it uses dramatic, carefully composed imagery, do not fill the video with generic AI pictures.

If it uses a consistent typography and animation language, maintain that language throughout.

The visual identity must be deliberate and consistent from the opening frame to the ending.

**Important originality requirement**

Use researched material as a visual and creative reference, not as material to duplicate without permission.

Create original compositions, footage selections, graphics and animations.

Do not reuse copyrighted competitor footage, distinctive proprietary graphics or protected branding without appropriate rights.

Do not jump directly from research into video generation.

Every video must first receive a complete production package.

For every scene specify:

- Narration.
- Visual description.

- Composition.
- Camera movement.
- Animation.
- Transition.
- Sound effects.
- Music.

5. **Asset preparation**

- Archival material with appropriate usage rights.
- Maps.
- Illustrations.
- Motion graphics.
- Titles.
- Backgrounds.

6. **Thumbnail and packaging**

- Thumbnail concept.
- Actual thumbnail artwork.
- Title.
- Description.
- Appropriate metadata.

## 8. VISUAL QUALITY CONTROL

Before full rendering, compare the newly prepared visual assets with the researched reference.

Check:

- Does the visual aesthetic match the reference's production principles?
- Is the image quality consistent?
- Is the colour treatment appropriate?
- Are compositions deliberate?
- Are animations consistent?
- Are transitions appropriate?
- Does every scene belong to the same visual identity?
- Does the opening look compelling?
- Does the visual material reinforce the narration?

For Shorts, prepare a first-five-to-ten-second preview.

For long-form, prepare representative scene previews and a visual contact sheet covering the major visual styles used throughout the video.

Do not proceed to full rendering until the required creative approval is obtained.
