# Story outline

Central question: What did the physical evidence show the Antikythera mechanism could actually do?

Thesis: The physical evidence from its gears and inscriptions shows that this 2nd century BC device could predict astronomical positions and eclipses decades in advance, and track athletic game cycles.

Why it matters: It demonstrates the advanced mechanical capabilities of ancient Greek technology.

Viewer starts knowing: The viewer knows an ancient artefact called the Antikythera mechanism exists.

Viewer ends understanding: The viewer understands the specific astronomical and calendar-tracking functions revealed by the physical components of the mechanism.

- b1 (opening, link: none, claims: c5): Establish the physical presence of gears and inscriptions in the fragments
- b2 (development, link: evidence, claims: c6): Show how the largest gear's physical structure supported complex counting
- b3 (payoff, link: explanation, claims: c8,c9): Reveal the direct physical functions of predicting eclipses and tracking game cycles
- b4 (ending, link: consequence, claims: none): Summarize what the physical evidence proves the device could do

Questions raised: "What functions were enabled by the gears and inscriptions found in the fragments?" raised in b1, answered in b3

Conclusion: Physical evidence proves the Antikythera mechanism could predict eclipses and track athletic cycles.
