## Claims

- **c1** [primary_source] Archduke Franz Ferdinand and Sophie are photographed getting into a car at Sarajevo City Hall on 28 June 1914, shortly before the assassination.  
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_in_Sarajevo,_June_1914_Q91848.jpg  
  Excerpt: "get into a motor car to depart from the City Hall, Sarajevo, shortly before they were assassinated"  
  Context: Photographer unknown; caption text is the Imperial War Museums description carried on Commons.
- **c2** [established_fact] Sarajevo is the capital of Bosnia and Herzegovina.  
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo  
  Excerpt: "capital and largest city of Bosnia and Herzegovina"  
  Context: Shown on a Natural Earth 1:50m map (public domain); place coordinates from Wikipedia.
- **c3** [established_fact] Bosnia and Herzegovina was formally annexed by Austria-Hungary in 1908, and was part of it in June 1914.  
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand  
  Excerpt: "Bosnia and Herzegovina, formally annexed by Austria-Hungary in 1908"  
  Context: The dated map is an 1895 ethnographic map by Richard Andree with the 1914 border added later; it is used to show the 1914 border, not 1895 politics.
- **c4** [primary_source] The New York Times front page of Monday 29 June 1914 reports the archduke and his wife were killed in Sarajevo by a Bosnian youth.  
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_New_York_Times_1914-06-29-_Vol_63_Iss_20610_(IA_per_new-york-times-magazine_the-new-york-times_1914-06-29_63_20610).pdf  
  Excerpt: "HEIR TO AUSTRIA'S THRONE IS SLAIN WITH HIS WIFE BY A BOSNIAN YOUTH TO AVENGE SEIZURE OF HIS COUNTRY"  
  Context: Headline read from the scanned page image. The newspaper's own framing (avenging seizure of a country) is the paper's, 1914.
- **c5** [later_interpretation] The photograph is often said to show the arrest of Gavrilo Princip, but several scholars say it shows Ferdinand Behr, a bystander initially suspected of involvement.  
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ferdinand_Behr_arrested_in_Sarajevo_1914.jpg  
  Excerpt: "often said to depict the arrest of Gavrilo Princip, although several scholars say that it depicts the arrest of Ferdinand Behr, a bystander"  
  Context: Cited on Commons to Finestone & Massie, The Last Courts of Europe (Dent, 1981, p. 247). Scholarly attribution, not a caption from 1914.
- **c6** [later_interpretation] David James Smith writes that there is no photograph of Princip's arrest.  
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ferdinand_Behr_arrested_in_Sarajevo_1914.jpg  
  Excerpt: "there is no photograph of Gavro's arrest - this photograph shows the arrest of Behr"  
  Context: Quoted on Commons from David James Smith, One Morning in Sarajevo (Hachette UK, 2010). One author's judgement; the narration attributes it.
