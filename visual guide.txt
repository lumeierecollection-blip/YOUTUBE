4. THE MUTE TEST
============================================================

For every visual beat:

Mute the narration.

Look at the frame.

Ask:

"What relationship does this picture communicate?"

If the answer is:

"There's a box with some words."

FAIL.

If the answer is:

"There are some arrows showing something."

FAIL.

If the answer is:

"It is a generic diagram about a process."

FAIL.

If the answer is:

"I can understand what is happening because the objects physically/visually behave according to the narrated mechanism."

PASS.

Every strategy must have a specific mute relationship.

============================================================
5. VISUALS MUST REPRESENT MECHANISMS
============================================================

Do not visualize nouns only.

Visualize the mechanism.

Example:

Narration:

"Police used a geofence warrant to scan a 150-meter radius."

BAD:

Huge "150 M" text.

BAD:

Circle + dots + label.

BETTER:

An actual spatial environment.

An origin.

A geographic field.

A boundary expanding through physical space.

Objects/devices existing at different distances.

The boundary crossing them.

Objects inside becoming selected.

Objects outside remaining outside.

Camera establishing the environment.

Camera moving toward the relevant region.

Measurement appearing as part of the environment.

The number "150 m" should be a dimension of the visual, not the visual itself.

The frame should explain the sentence without requiring the viewer to read.

============================================================
11. CAMERA — THIS IS CRITICAL
============================================================

The camera must NOT simply:

zoom in slowly
OR
zoom out slowly
OR
lerp from point A to point B for the entire beat.

The Liamrjohnston reference explicitly rejects "slow zoom as the only camera idea."

Use:

HOLD
→
MOVE
→
HOLD
→
MOVE
→
SETTLE

The camera should behave like an editor choosing what the audience sees.

For example:

0–0.6 sec:
establish object/action

0.6–1.4 sec:
hold while action happens

1.4–1.8 sec:
camera travels toward consequence

1.8–2.8 sec:
hold on consequence

2.8–3.2 sec:
small settle

3.2–4.0 sec:
payoff

These are illustrative.

Actual timing MUST come from the beat's real SRT window.

Every camera move needs an answer to:

"Why is the camera moving?"

Valid:

- reveal a consequence
- follow an object
- move from cause to effect
- reveal scale
- move toward evidence
- reveal the other side of a comparison
- move from detail to context
- move from context to detail
- follow physical motion

Invalid:

- "to make it cinematic"
- "because the frame felt static"
- "because movement is good"
- arbitrary zoom
- arbitrary pan

Movement must explain.
