# Reference study: Paper Pitch and The Football Realm

Written 2026-11-27 for the Rival Lines channel. Everything here is observed from the channels' public metadata, thumbnails, avatar and banner, and from frame contact sheets of the three Paper Pitch videos (one frame every 4 to 5 seconds, with the words spoken at that moment). No transcript, audio or frame is stored in this repo; only derived observations and short phrases.

Limits, stated plainly:
- Paper Pitch was studied from frames and metadata. Its audio was not listened to by a person; music is taken from the creator's own credit line, not from recognition.
- The Football Realm was studied from channel metadata, thumbnails and one video's audio only. Its videos were not frame-studied.
- View counts, dates and subscriber numbers are as read on 2026-11-27 and change.
- "What works" below is inferred from one breakout (926,857 views) and two videos that did not break out (3,290 and 3,443 views). Three videos cannot separate the format from the topic or from luck. The 1,000-goals topic itself is probably most of the reach.

Treat the look as an established style, not as Paper Pitch's invention.

## 2. How a video is built (Paper Pitch)

Shape, the same in all three:
1. Cold open on one concrete number or event, in the first 5 seconds. Examples: a count that is "15 short of 1,000"; "4 goals, no win"; two point totals shown side by side with a vertical divider.
2. The question is stated as the title, then restated on screen with the title card around the 60 second mark in the Ballon d'Or video.
3. A premise that makes the question measurable ("32 careers", "30 names", "31 seasons").
4. Chapters of roughly 30 to 60 seconds, each answering one sub-question. Each chapter has a small lowercase serif title top-left and a punchy phrase: "the same age.", "thirty-two careers.", "a thousand, before.", "the strict count.", "the slow start.", "the number is 40.", "after thirty.", "the second career.", "the one who will.", "the answer." (1,000 goals); "the table.", "the 119.", "the ghosts.", "expected goals.", "the defense.", "the excuse.", "survival.", "10,000 seasons.", "a simple model.", "the question.", "above the pitch.", "the blame." (Man United); "the machine.", "lane three.", "the price of the ticket.", "one rule.", "sixth place.", "your vote." (Ballon d'Or).
5. One dark chapter for the turn (a darker background for the cost, the villain or the surprise), then back to cream for the answer. The Ballon d'Or video runs dark for the first third and the "price" chapters, cream for the reasoning chapters.
6. "The answer" restates the headline number in one frame.
7. Closing card that asks the viewer a specific question ("who gets closest?", "who was your Ballon d'Or 2026?", "who do you think is most to blame?"). This is the comment engine: the 1,000-goals video has exactly 1,000 comments.

Voice: first person, plain, conversational, no hype words, numbers spoken precisely. It sounds like one person explaining a spreadsheet they built, not a pundit. Sentences state a number and then say what it means. It names its method ("we took every winner since 1995", "I put together a model") and says what the model cannot see.

Cut rate: low. Scene-change detection found 15 cuts in 552 s, 35 in 557 s and 3 in 643 s. Almost nothing cuts; the story advances by the same chart changing state (a bar appears, a line extends, a row highlights, a second series enters). A new idea gets a new composition, usually with a soft fade through the background.

## 3. Motion-graphics grammar

- Background: cream paper grain for reasoning, near-black for tension or cost, sometimes with a very large dim object (the Ballon d'Or itself) behind the content.
- Type: lowercase serif for chapter titles and annotations (Playfair-like), bold sans for numbers and labels. One word in a title picks up the accent colour.
- Palette: a single crimson accent, plus black and cream; gold for awards; green or blue only for a second series. Colour is functional (the 18th row of the table is red, the winner's bar is gold).
- Numbers count up. Stat blocks pair a big number with a tiny unit label and a small badge ("1st", "6th", "final").
- Players: transparent cutouts with a thin offset colour shadow in the club's colour, or circular portrait chips in rows (the 31 Ballon d'Or winners as a two-row grid of chips, filtered or struck through as the argument proceeds).
- Charts, all built to answer one question each: two-bar comparison with a divider; dot or ball matrices (1 dot = 1 season or 1 goal); growth curve against a dashed target line with a checkered finish; histogram of simulated outcomes with the real value marked; league table with one highlighted row; timeline lanes (four weeks against eleven months drawn as a strip of blocks); a vote-weight bar ladder (15, 12, 10, 8, 7, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1); and a persistent side widget (a "chance of going down" thermometer) that updates across chapters.
- Evidence cards: article, tweet, ESPN and Sky Sports screenshots, with a tape-corner polaroid or torn-edge frame. A crossed-out photo means "the model cannot see this".
- Photos: full frame with a blurred copy behind; portrait photos pillarboxed. Real photographs only for people and places.
- Footage: rare and short. Torn-edge, tinted or inside a retro TV frame, a few seconds each, always while the narration is about that specific moment. The 1,000-goals video uses essentially none; the Man United video uses a handful.
- Pacing inside a scene: elements enter in the order the sentence names them. The viewer is never looking at something the narrator has not reached yet.

## 4. Footage and photo use: what is said when

- A photo or clip appears when the narration names the person or event, and leaves when the sentence moves on. It is never wallpaper.
- Footage is never used to carry an argument. The argument is in the chart; the clip is the "this is who we mean" beat.
- Every claim card carries a small source line. The description carries the long source list.
- Transformative test that this format passes and that Rival Lines must also pass: the video's content is its own question, its own counting and its own chart. Third-party media is a few seconds of illustration under original commentary, credited and described as commentary.

## 5. Titles

Pattern: a "How / Could / Is" question in Title Case, ending with a question mark, no capitals-for-emphasis, no clickbait adjectives, 6 to 9 words. The title is a question a casual fan has asked, and the video's own question. Examples: "How Hard Is It To Score 1,000 Goals?", "How To Win the Ballon d'Or?", "Could Manchester United Actually Get Relegated?". "Actually" is the one intensifier used.

Football Realm titles are different: declarative and emotional ("... Was Right All Along.", "Nobody Listened."). Do not mix the two.

## 6. Thumbnails

Paper Pitch formula:
- 16:9, soft white or pale grey gradient (black and gold for the Ballon d'Or).
- One cutout player on one side, with a motion-blur trail fading into the background.
- A short lowercase serif headline with one bold keyword ("not **enough.**", "just do **this.**", "the **drop?**").
- One mini data visual that is the video's answer shape: a curve against a 1,000 line, a trophy with a "? goals" tag, a table with the 18th row red.
- A hand-drawn red arrow with an italic serif annotation ("Haaland, 26").
- A small "PP" badge top-left.
- No shocked faces, no neon, no more than about 5 words of headline.

## 7. SEO: tags, descriptions, hashtags

Tags: 17 to 21 per video. Entity tags first (players, clubs, "Ronaldo 1000 goals", "Ballon d'Or 2026"), then topic phrases, then four tags that end every video: football analysis, football data, football explained, and the channel name.

Description template, in order:
1. A hook paragraph with specific numbers and a first-person method sentence ("I put together", "we went through").
2. One comment-bait question naming the candidates.
3. "Numbers are correct to <date>."
4. Chapter list. The chapter titles in the description mirror the on-screen chapter cards, in Title Case ("15 short of 1,000", "The Slow Start", "The Number Is 40", "What Happens After 30", "The Answer"). The Man United video has 9 chapters.
5. A disclaimer: not affiliated with or endorsed by any club, league or competition.
6. Data section naming each source (football-data.co.uk, ESPN, Sky Sports and others).
7. Images section: licence and author via Wikimedia Commons for CC photos, plus a fair-use sentence for press and match images ("commentary and analysis").
8. "Match footage: short clips used for commentary and analysis."
9. Music credit line. Paper Pitch credits "Illusions", "Stand" and "Shadows" by Anno Domini Beats, from the YouTube Audio Library. This is the creator's credit; recognition was not run on it.

Hashtags: Paper Pitch uses none in the description. Football Realm uses a long keyword tag list and short news-style descriptions. For Rival Lines, the supported choice is no hashtags, or at most three at the very end; there is no evidence here that hashtags helped.

## 8. Profile picture and banner

Avatar: 900 by 900, cream paper grain, a bold black high-contrast serif "PP", a hand-drawn red underline stroke beneath. Readable at 48 pixels because it is two letters and one stroke.

Banner: 2560 by 424, torn-paper collage panels in yellow, red, green and blue with white-outlined player cutouts, a serif wordmark with the same red underline, and a subscribe call to action with a cookie illustration.

Rival Lines should keep the structure (two letters, one stroke, paper grain) and use its own letters and accent. It must not copy the "PP" mark or the cookie.

## 9. What it takes to get to a Paper Pitch result

What can be repeated: a number-first cold open; one question per video; a model or count that is the channel's own; chapters titled in lowercase phrases; one accent colour; charts that change state instead of cutting; a closing question; complete source and licence credits.

What cannot be promised: the 926K views. The Ballon d'Or and Man United videos, built in the same grammar, got about 3,300 views each. Topic timing (a headline milestone) was probably the difference. Plan on topic selection as a separate lever from format.

## 10. Rival Lines episode 1 against this grammar

State found 2026-11-27, branch `render/short-26-rewrite`, run 45983080784: the episode renders (stills and full video both succeed in Actions, artifacts `rival-lines-stills-4` and `rival-lines-video-4`). It has 32 scenes over 414.7 s of narration, a research and model folder, a gate (`gate-episode.mjs`), and a Remotion render. It has only narration audio.

Matches: cream paper, a single accent, tape-corner photos, evidence-style stat cards, a dot matrix, a bar-ladder, a probability curve against a dated axis with a dark chart scene, a closing question ("which count would you trust"), source line on every scene, a data and images credit scene.

Gaps against the reference:
1. No music bed and no sound effects. Reference videos have a bed under the narration; the audio-library work in `channels/rival-lines/audio/` is meant to fill this.
2. One still composition per beat, 32 scenes in 415 s, about 13 s each. The reference changes a chart's state inside one scene. The stills show each beat as a single finished card, so within-scene build order should be checked in the video, not the stills.
3. Chapter titles. Reference chapters have a lowercase serif title and a punchy phrase on screen; ep1 chapter data exists (`chapter.title`, for example "the number") but the stills do not show them top-left on every scene. Check in the video.
4. No cutout players with an offset colour shadow; ep1 uses rectangular tape photos only. Cutouts need background removal on licensed photos; an offline tool and a licence check are needed.
5. No footage clips. The reference uses almost none for this topic, so this is not a gap for ep1.
6. Dark chapter used once for the model curves; the reference uses it for tension. Fine for this topic.
7. Closing card is present; the comment question could name the options (for example the three totals) as the reference does.
8. Title, description, tags and thumbnail: not yet produced. The formulas above apply.

Topic note: ep1's topic (can Ronaldo reach 1,000, with odds by date) overlaps Paper Pitch's breakout topic. Its angle is different (a dated probability from its own model, and the sources disagreeing on the total), which is what keeps it transformative. Its narration and charts must stay its own.
