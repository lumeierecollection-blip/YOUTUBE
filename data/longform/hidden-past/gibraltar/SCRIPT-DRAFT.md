## Cold open
In 1969, Spain closed its border with Gibraltar. It cut off all contact and severely restricted movement. A rock of a few square kilometres, at the
tip of Spain, was sealed off from the country it is attached to. The border would not fully reopen until February 1985.
Spain had already tried to take it back by force, in 1727, and in 1779, in the longest siege the British armed forces have ever endured:
three years, seven months and twelve days. Both sides fired nearly half a million rounds of shot. And Gibraltar stayed British.
So how does a country fail, for more than three hundred years, to recover a piece of land on its own coast?
The usual answer is the British Navy. And that's not wrong, exactly. But it can't be the whole answer, because the moment that decided it came
from neither navy. It came from 12,138 people with a pencil.
(Promise: a treaty clause written "in perpetuity", the longest siege in British military history, a dictator's border closure, and two votes
that weren't even close.)
