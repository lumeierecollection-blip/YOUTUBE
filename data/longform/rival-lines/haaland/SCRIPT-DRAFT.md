## Hook
In the 2022-23 season, Erling Haaland played 35 Premier League games. He scored 36 goals. No player in the history of the Premier League had ever
scored that many in one season. Across every competition, he scored 52 in 53. Another record.
And here's the strange part. He isn't a striker who is always on the ball. Even the descriptions of his game say he is less involved in possession than
a typical striker. The player who touches the ball the least is the one who scores the most.
So what is it? Is it his size? He's one metre ninety-five. Is it speed? Is it luck? Or is there something about the way he plays that nobody else
in the league is doing?
Well, we took a look at what actually makes Haaland so good, and the answers were pretty interesting, to say the least.

## 1. The pace of the records
Before we get to why, we need to see how far ahead he really is.
In his first month in England, he scored a hat-trick against Crystal Palace, then another, four days later, against Nottingham Forest. That made
him the fastest player in Premier League history to two hat-tricks. He beat the old record by fourteen matches.
By October, he had three. Michael Owen had needed 48 league games to score three hat-tricks. Haaland needed eight.
By the end of December, he had twenty Premier League goals in fourteen games. The previous record, Kevin Phillips', took seven more.
By January, he had 25 goals in 19 games. That was already more than the previous season's top scorers, Mohamed Salah and Son Heung-min, had scored
in the whole season: 23 each.
And it didn't stop. He reached 50 Premier League goals in 48 games. Andy Cole's record had been 65. He reached 100 league goals across Europe's
top five leagues in 103 appearances. The previous record, set by Ronaldo, was 133.
These aren't small margins. He isn't breaking records by a game or two. He's halving them.

## 2. The body
So let's start with the obvious answer. Haaland is one metre ninety-five tall, and strong with it. Descriptions of his game call him a player who uses
his sizeable frame to hold the ball up and bring others into the game, protecting it with his back to goal.
But plenty of big strikers have played in the Premier League. What's unusual is that his size comes with speed. He has the pace to run in behind,
and he can carry the ball at pace on the counter-attack.
And it showed up early. When he was five years old, he reportedly set a world record for his age in the standing long jump.
Jurgen Klopp put it this way: physically, he sets new standards. The combination of being really physical, technical, and his sensational awareness.

## 3. The movement
Which brings us to the part you can't see in a highlight reel.
Inside the box, Haaland makes small, sharp movements. He looks for the moment a teammate can find him in space, then changes the line he's
running on and accelerates into it. That's what makes him so hard for defenders to read.
Klopp again: he always knows where the decisive gaps are. Barely offside.
And when he isn't in the box, he does something most strikers his size don't. He drops deep to collect the ball, spreads it wide, then turns and
sprints towards goal. Sometimes he drops so deep that defenders can't follow him at all.

## 4. Doing less
This is where the puzzle from the start comes back.
Haaland is less involved in possession than a typical striker. But his calmness, patience, timing and movement off the ball have a huge impact on
how his team builds attacks. He doesn't need fifty touches. He needs the right two.
And when he does get the ball, his aim is always the same: get a shot off. He can finish with either foot, and with his head.
In March 2023, against RB Leipzig, he scored five goals in one Champions League match, in 57 minutes. Only Lionel Messi and Luiz Adriano had ever
scored five in a single game in that competition.

## 5. It was always there
None of this started in Manchester.
At the 2019 Under-20 World Cup, he scored nine goals in a single match. A record.
At Red Bull Salzburg, he scored 28 goals in 22 appearances in half a season.
On his debut for Borussia Dortmund, he came off the bench and scored a hat-trick in 23 minutes. He became the first player to score seven goals in
his first three Bundesliga games, and the fastest to fifteen Champions League goals, in twelve matches.


## 6. The first season, in full
Go back to that first season in England and look at it month by month.
In his first month, August 2022, he was voted Premier League Player of the Month.
On the 2nd of October, against Manchester United, he became the first player in Premier League history to score a hat-trick in three
successive home matches, and he set up two more goals in a 6-3 derby win.
In March, he broke Tommy Johnson's club record of 38 goals in a season, a record that had stood since 1928-29. Four days later, against
Burnley in the FA Cup, he scored his sixth hat-trick of the season and passed 40 goals.
Then he passed 44, the record across all competitions for a Premier League player, held by Mohamed Salah and Ruud van Nistelrooy.
On the 3rd of May, against West Ham, he scored his 35th league goal, passing Alan Shearer and Andy Cole's joint record for a single season.
He was named the Football Writers' Footballer of the Year by a record margin: 82 percent of the vote, ahead of Arsenal's Bukayo Saka and Martin Odegaard.
And by setting up a goal for Phil Foden against Brighton on the 24th of May, he reached 44 goals and assists combined in the league, equalling
Thierry Henry's record for a 38-game Premier League season.
He finished with the Golden Boot, and the European Golden Shoe, for the top league scorer in Europe.

## 7. Then he did it again
It would be easy to call one season a fluke. So look at the next one.
In August 2023, his first three goals of the season made him the fastest player ever to 100 league goals in Europe's top five leagues.
In October, he was given the Gerd Muller Trophy, for the most goals for club and country in a season.
In November, against Liverpool, he reached 50 Premier League goals in 48 games.
A week later, he scored five goals in one FA Cup game against Luton Town. It was the second time he had scored five in a game for City, and
he became the first player to do it twice. It also matched the club's record for goals in an FA Cup match, set in 1926 and 1930.
At City, he has won two Premier League titles and three Golden Boots.

## 8. Before Manchester
And the pattern was there in Germany, too.
At Dortmund, he became the first Bundesliga player to score five goals in his first two matches, in just 56 minutes on the pitch.
In the Champions League, he became the fastest and youngest player to reach 20 goals in the competition, in only 14 matches, and the first
to score more than once in four Champions League games in a row.
When he scored his 50th Bundesliga goal, he did it in fewer appearances than anyone before him, and he was the youngest player to get there.

## Verdict
So why is Haaland so good? It's not one thing. It's the size of a target man with the pace of a winger. It's movement that defenders can't read.
And it's a striker who knows that the touch that matters is the last one.
Gary Neville called him truly unique. FIFPRO called him the deadliest striker in football today.
The numbers say something simpler. Every record he goes after, he doesn't just break. He halves.
