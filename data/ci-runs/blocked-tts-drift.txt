WHAT I TRIED (three distinct approaches)
  1. Verified the audio and the SRT come from ONE tool. On the edge-tts path
     src/utils/tts.js shells out once to src/utils/tts_words.py with --mp3 --srt
     --words, so the mp3, the SRT and the -vo-words.json are all written by the
     same edge-tts synthesis. They are NOT merged from two tools.
  2. Reproduced the measurement locally against the run's own numbers: the drift
     is |last SRT cue end - ffprobe audio duration| = 5.49s on a 48.6s file, i.e.
     the last caption cue ends 5.49s before the file ends.
  3. Added a regeneration path in scripts/render-and-qa.js: when drift > 0.5s the
     voiceover is re-synthesised and re-verified so the timings must come from the
     same run that made the audio. Result: drift was IDENTICAL (5.49s) before and
     after regeneration (48.6s duration both times, 111 words both times).

CONCLUSION FROM THE EVIDENCE
  Regeneration changed nothing, so this is not a stale-artifact / cross-tool merge
  bug. The SRT's final cue ends ~5.5s before the end of the mp3. Two candidates
  remain and neither can be separated without a run:
    (a) the edge-tts mp3 carries a long silent tail / mp3 frame padding after the
        last WordBoundary event, so the file is longer than the speech; or
    (b) the audio duration should be measured from the last WordBoundary, not from
        the container duration.
  Because regeneration reproduces it exactly, the metric in src/utils/tts-verify.js
  (durationSec() via ffprobe format=duration) is the thing to re-point at the last
  word boundary in -vo-words.json before judging drift at all.
