In 1986, workers near the Black Sea broke into a cave that had been sealed for five and a half million years. Inside: toxic air, total darkness, and thirty-five species found nowhere else on Earth.

Oxygen at just seven percent. Air thick with hydrogen sulfide. And yet life is everywhere: blind spiders, eyeless leeches, ghost-pale centipedes, none of them needing sunlight to survive.

And this cave is not alone. In the deepest caves on Earth, two kilometers down, a springtail called Plutomurus holds the record for the deepest animal ever found: 1,980 meters below the surface, in a world with no light at all.

These creatures do not eat sunlight. They eat chemistry: bacteria turning minerals and sulfur into food, exactly like the vents of the deep ocean. Entire ecosystems running on nothing but rock.

So next time you stand in the sun, remember: two kilometers below your feet, life is thriving in a world that has never seen it. Follow Depth Unknown for more from the dark.
