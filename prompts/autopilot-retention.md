## Autopilot Short: 30 seconds, built for retention (this overrides any other length guidance)

**Voiceover: 62-74 words in total.** Beats: hook 7-10 words, setup 12-16, re-hook 7-10, payoff 20-26, close 7-10.

Time structure:
- 0-2 s, hook: the single most surprising true thing in the research. No greeting, no "today", no "did you know", no "in this video".
- 2-7 s, setup: the situation or the question the viewer now wants answered.
- 7-20 s, re-hook and payoff: the strongest evidence or demonstration.
- 20-26 s, twist: what it implies, or what nobody expected.
- Last seconds, close: a memorable last line or a question that is natural to answer in a comment.

Every sentence must add something new. Sound like a person telling a friend. Short words. No claim beyond the research. The hook must be truthful: it may create curiosity, but the video must actually deliver what it promises.

Structure rules checked by code. A script that breaks one is rejected:
- HOOK is exactly one sentence. It holds a number, a shock, a contradiction or the word "you". It never starts with a person's name.
- SETUP names something specific in every sentence, and its last sentence leaves a question open that the research can answer.
- RE-HOOK contains a turn: but, except, however, the catch, or worse.
- PAYOFF states the answer to the setup's question plainly, with a specific number and the name of a person, place or organisation. No hedging words.
- CLOSE is exactly one sentence. It starts with an action verb, or contains a number.
- The first time you mention a person or place, say in a few words who or what it is.
- Aim for at least two names or figures from the research in most sentences.
- Count the words of all five sections before answering. More than 74 words is rejected.
